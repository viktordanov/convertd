# Overhead benchmark (2026-11-26)

The overhead gate (5 ms at p50, `src/README.md`, Measurement), measured on the real image with warm workers.

**Result: the gate passes.** For the one-page DOCX to PDF, `overhead` is 0.883 ms at p50, against a limit of 5 ms. With the service's own checks, `overhead_strict` is 1.736 ms at p50.

## Definitions

All figures come from the `Server-Timing` header of each answer, except the client column.

- `overhead` = `total` − `queue` − (`convert_engine` + `inspect_engine` + `probe_engine` + `render_engine`). This is the gate: p50, warm one-page DOCX, `output=pdf`.
- `overhead_strict` = `overhead` + `inspect_engine` + `probe_engine`: what the service's own checks cost.
- `share_supervisor` = `total` − (`convert` + `inspect` + `render`): the supervisor's part.
- `share_workers` = Σ (stage wall − stage engine time) − `queue`. It holds lease, IPC and the worker's work outside the engine, such as the office worker's pre-flight scan.
- `client`: the wall time that the client saw. It includes Docker Desktop's port forward on macOS and is not part of the gate.

## Method

1. Inputs, checked against the SHA-256 values in `corpus/expected.json` before the run:
   - `docx-one-page` (`out/docx/one-page.docx`, 36 975 bytes);
   - `pdf-text3` (`out/pdf/text3.pdf`, 1 360 bytes).
2. The runner started the container and waited for `/readyz` to answer 200, then 10 s more for the pools' warm-up.
3. Three cases, in turn, three times:
   - `docx-one-page` to `output=pdf` (the gate);
   - `pdf-text3` to `output=pdf`;
   - `docx-one-page` to `output=bundle`, `{"format":"jpeg","dpi":72}`.
4. Each case in each run: 200 warm-up requests that were not measured, then 2 000 measured requests. That is 6 000 measured requests per case and 19 800 requests in total.

Every one of the 19 800 conversions answered 200 (server access log). No request started a worker on its path (`spawn` and `bind` were 0), and no request waited more than 0.1 ms in the queue. The log shows 749 `worker ready` lines: `WORKER_MAX_JOBS=50` replaced workers often, but always off the request path.

**The one 503 in the run log** is from the runner's wait for readiness, before the first request. The first `curl /readyz` came while the pools were still starting, and `/readyz` answered 503 `starting`. The workers were ready at 09:30:55.69, the next poll one second later got 200, and `/v1/capabilities` answered at 09:30:56.53. No conversion answered 503.

## Raw data

The runner wrote one JSON line per measured request, with every stage in microseconds. That made nine files of 0.5 to 0.8 MB, 5.9 MB in total. With them were `server.log` (15 MB), `capabilities.json` and `conditions.txt`. They are not kept, in the repository or anywhere else. `examples/overhead.rs --report <files>` rebuilds the pooled tables from such files.

The script that ran the benchmark is not kept either. Its steps are the Method above. It checks the inputs and the machine, starts and warms up the container, runs the three cases three times, and pools the tables.
